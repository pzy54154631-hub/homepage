About Me

Hello! I am Ziyu Peng, an undergraduate student in Statistics and Data Science at the University of Colorado Boulder.

Research Interests

  • Reliable multimodal reasoning. I study how models identify referents, maintain structured visual representations, compare candidate answers, and decide when to answer, clarify, or abstain. I use VQA, ChartQA, and abstract visual reasoning to investigate uncertainty, calibration, selective prediction, and computational ideas from predictive processing and human learning.
  • Efficient AI inference systems. I am interested in how routing, scheduling, caching, and context management can make conditional multimodal and agentic pipelines more efficient and reliable, especially when inference unfolds over multiple stages and intermediate context grows.

Projects

Ambiguity Is Not One Scalar: Explicit Referents Improve VQA Routing

This project develops a two-stage VQA router over ANSWER / CLARIFY / ABSTAIN. For uncertain or focus-sensitive inputs, it explicitly enumerates plausible referents instead of treating ambiguity as a single confidence score.

On a 26,425-example diagnostic benchmark, referent enumeration recovered 20/22 audited low-score focus-ambiguity false negatives, versus 0/22 for answer self-consistency.

Topics: VQA, conditional multimodal inference, selective prediction, calibration

View project on GitHub

PW-OFPC: Precision-Weighted Object-File Predictive Coding

This project builds an uncertainty-aware abstract visual reasoning pipeline that preserves object-file posteriors and compares answer candidates jointly using predictive-coding residuals.

On public RAVEN, the inference ladder improved macro accuracy from 0.537 to 0.857. Controlled experiments separated object-formation failures from residual and candidate-selection failures.

Topics: uncertainty-aware inference, object-centric reasoning, predictive coding, candidate comparison

Manuscripts & Writing

Scale Is Not Symmetric: Directional Scale Reliability in Chart Question Answering

Submitted manuscript, 2026.

This work studies a reliability problem in chart question answering: multimodal models may identify a numerically related value but report it at the wrong scale, such as answering 0.61 when the chart question requires 61. It treats this behavior as a directional scale-regime selection failure rather than a generic numerical mistake.

The manuscript introduces ScaleQA-Natural, a 520-example audited diagnostic set for decimal/percent and unit-scale conflicts, and evaluates prompting, directional LoRA adapters, verifiers, calculators, likelihood scoring, and policy selectors.

A targeted adapter improved held-out ChartQA accuracy from 88.60% to 89.32% and recovered 49.52% of strict scale errors; broader diagnostics identified regime selection as the main remaining bottleneck.

Topics: chart question answering, multimodal reliability, LoRA, policy selection

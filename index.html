About Me

Hello! I am Ziyu Peng, an undergraduate student in Statistics and Data Science at the University of Colorado Boulder.

My research interests center on multimodal and abstract visual reasoning, uncertainty-aware inference, and reliable AI. I work on VQA routing, calibration, selective prediction, object-centric representations, predictive coding, and candidate comparison.

I am also interested in the systems side of conditional multimodal reasoning, especially serving, scheduling, and caching, and in how ideas from human learning, representation, and predictive processing can inform artificial intelligence.

Project

Scale Is Not Symmetric: Directional Scale Reliability in Chart Question Answering

This project studies a reliability problem in chart question answering: multimodal models may identify a numerically related value but report it at the wrong scale, such as answering 0.61 when the chart question requires 61. The project treats this behavior as a scale-regime selection failure involving chart units, axes, question wording, and answer conventions, rather than as a generic numerical mistake.

The work investigates whether scale correction should be treated as a directional and regime-dependent problem rather than as unconditional numerical normalization. It analyzes scale-related failures in chart QA and uses diagnostic probes including LoRA scale-contrastive adapters, prompting strategies, structured verifiers, calculators, and lightweight policy selectors.

The project introduces and analyzes ScaleQA-Natural, a diagnostic set of natural scale-conflict candidates. It studies when a model should keep its original answer, switch to a larger-scale answer, switch to a smaller-scale answer, or rely on unit and axis evidence. The main finding is that generating a scaled alternative is often easier than selecting the correct scale regime.

Topics: chart question answering, multimodal AI, numerical reliability, scale inconsistency, LoRA adapters, policy selection

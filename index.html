About Me

Hello! I am Ziyu Peng, an undergraduate student in Statistics at the University of Colorado Boulder.

My academic interests lie at the intersection of statistics, data science, artificial intelligence, and computational neuroscience. I am especially interested in multimodal reasoning, vision-language models, NeuroAI, representation analysis, and how computational models can be compared with human cognition and brain data.

I am currently building a strong foundation in statistics, computer science, machine learning, and scientific computing. In the long term, I hope to pursue research in computational neuroscience, interpretable machine learning, and human-centered artificial intelligence.

Selected Projects

Multilingual Reasoning in Large Language Models

I am exploring how large language models perform reasoning across different languages, with a focus on hidden representations, chain-of-thought behavior, and test-time reasoning efficiency.

Vision Foundation Models and Human Visual Representations

This project investigates how representations from vision foundation models such as CLIP, DINOv2, ViT, and related architectures align with human visual behavior and brain data. Potential methods include RSA, CKA, and linear probing.

Academic Paper Survey Tool

I am developing a literature mining tool for collecting, filtering, summarizing, and organizing research papers from sources such as arXiv, Semantic Scholar, OpenReview, and Google Scholar.

ROS2 Robotic Arm Control

I have worked on ROS2-based robotic arm control, including service interfaces, hardware communication, and master-slave teleoperation for a rover arm system.
